# Episode: Shreyas Doshi on Good vs. Great Product Managers, The LNO Framework, and High Agency
**Guest:** Shreyas Doshi (Product Leader at Stripe, Twitter, Google)
**Host:** Lenny Rachitsky
**Date:** 2023-08-20
**Topic:** Product Management Excellence, The LNO Framework, Pre-Mortems, High Agency

---

### Segment 1: Good vs. Great Product Managers [00:06:15]
Lenny: Shreyas, your tweet threads on Product Management have been read by millions. What is the fundamental difference between a good product manager and a truly great product manager?

Shreyas Doshi: A good product manager is an exceptional execution machine. They run sprints on time, communicate with engineers, keep JIRA clean, and deliver features according to the roadmap. But a great product manager obsesses over the question: *Are we building the right thing at all?* Good PMs focus on delivery; great PMs focus on leverage and outcomes. Great PMs understand that 90% of product features generate zero user impact. They spend 80% of their energy on the 10% of high-leverage decisions that alter the trajectory of the company.

### Segment 2: The LNO Framework for Energy & Task Management [00:18:40]
Lenny: Tell us about the LNO framework that so many executives now use.

Shreyas Doshi: Most people treat all tasks as equally deserving of excellence. That is a recipe for burnout and mediocrity. I categorize all work into three buckets:
1. Leverage (L): Tasks where great execution produces 10x to 100x return. Examples: defining product strategy, hiring core leads, crafting key product positioning. You must give these tasks 100% of your energy and obsess over every detail.
2. Neutral (N): Tasks where good execution is sufficient and great execution is wasteful. Examples: routine status reports, weekly sprint planning, standard feature specs. Do these well and move on.
3. Overhead (O): Tasks that just need to get done so you don't get fired or blocked. Examples: compliance approvals, routine administrative forms. Do them at a 'passing grade' as quickly as humanly possible, delegate them, or automate them.

If you spend your best morning brainpower on Overhead tasks, you have already lost the day.

---

### Segment 3: High Agency & Pre-Mortems [00:32:10]
Lenny: What is the most underrated skill in product leadership?

Shreyas Doshi: High agency. High agency is the refusal to accept that the current circumstances dictate your outcome. When a high-agency PM hits an organizational blocker—legal says no, engineering estimates 6 months—they don't throw their hands up. They ask: *What would have to be true for this to happen in 2 weeks?*
And the second tool is the Pre-Mortem. Before shipping any major release, bring the whole team together and say: "Imagine it is six months from now, and this product launch has been an utter catastrophe. What went wrong?" This surfaces the unspoken fears that everyone knows but nobody felt safe to voice.
