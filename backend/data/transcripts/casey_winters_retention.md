# Episode: Casey Winters on Retention, Engagement, and Growth Loops
**Guest:** Casey Winters (Chief Product Officer at Eventbrite, former Growth Lead at Pinterest and Grubhub)
**Host:** Lenny Rachitsky
**Date:** 2023-05-10
**Topic:** Retention Curves, Engagement Loops, Churn Prevention, Growth Engineering

---

### Segment 1: Why Retention is the Only Metric That Really Matters [00:04:45]
Lenny: Casey, you have advised dozens of top tech companies on growth. What is the single biggest mistake early growth teams make?

Casey Winters: The biggest mistake is optimizing top-of-funnel acquisition before retention has flattened. If your retention curve goes to zero, pouring money or effort into acquisition is just filling a leaky bucket.
Retention is the foundation of growth. If 100 users sign up and by Day 30 only 2 users remain, you do not have a growth problem—you have a product-market fit problem. You must see the cohort retention curve flatten out parallel to the x-axis. That flat line represents your sticky, core audience.

---

### Segment 2: Identifying the Core Aha! Moment vs Cargo-Cult Metrics [00:19:20]
Lenny: Everyone talks about Facebook's famous '7 friends in 10 days'. How do teams actually find their true Aha moment?

Casey Winters: Most teams pick a metric that is correlational rather than causal. For example, at Grubhub, users who ordered 3 times in their first month had 80% annual retention. But forcing someone to order 3 times through aggressive coupons didn't create a retained user; it just subsidized their habit.
A real Aha moment occurs when a user experiences the core value proposition of your product for the first time. For Pinterest, it wasn't just saving a pin; it was returning the next week and discovering related ideas based on their saved pin. That proved they understood Pinterest as a personal inspiration engine.

---

### Segment 3: Growth Loops vs Funnels [00:33:15]
Lenny: Explain why modern growth strategy has shifted from traditional funnels to growth loops.

Casey Winters: Funnels are linear: Traffic $\rightarrow$ Sign up $\rightarrow$ Purchase. You put 100 users in the top, get 2 buyers at the bottom, and then you have to buy another 100 users to repeat it. That model gets exponentially more expensive over time.
A growth loop is circular: The output of one cohort of users serves as the input for the next cohort of users. For example, when a user pins a photo on Pinterest, that pin is indexed by Google SEO, which brings in 3 new users, who pin more photos. If your product doesn't reinvest user activity into new acquisition or retention, you will eventually hit a ceiling on growth.
