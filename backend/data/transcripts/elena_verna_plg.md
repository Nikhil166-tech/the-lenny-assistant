# Episode: Elena Verna on B2B Product-Led Growth, Viral Loops, and Freemium
**Guest:** Elena Verna (Head of Growth at Dropbox, Amplitude, Miro advisor)
**Host:** Lenny Rachitsky
**Date:** 2023-11-15
**Topic:** Product-Led Growth (PLG), B2B Freemium, Viral Loops, and Sales-Assist

---

### Segment 1: The Core Definition of B2B Product-Led Growth [00:04:12]
Lenny: Elena, welcome back. Let's start with the basics because people use PLG as a buzzword. What is real B2B product-led growth?

Elena Verna: PLG is not a go-to-market motion that replaces sales. That is the number one misconception. PLG is a distribution strategy where the product itself drives acquisition, retention, and expansion. In B2B, what that means is you acquire the end-user first before you ever talk to the buyer. In traditional sales-led growth (SLG), you pitch the VP of IT or CMO, they sign an enterprise contract, and force employees to adopt the software. In PLG, an individual designer, engineer, or PM signs up for free, falls in love with the product, invites three coworkers, and creates a bottom-up groundswell. The company is forced to purchase because the employees are already using it.

---

### Segment 2: Freemium vs. Free Trial — How to Choose [00:14:30]
Lenny: How should founders choose between a freemium model and a 14-day free trial?

Elena Verna: Here is the golden rule: If your product has network effects or viral loops, you must use Freemium. If you are Miro, Figma, Notion, or Slack, putting a 14-day time limit kills the viral distribution loop. You need users to stay on the free tier for months so they can invite collaborators, share boards, and share documents. Free trials are only good when you have high infrastructure cost per user, or when your product is single-player and delivers instant time-to-value within 48 hours without network effects.

---

### Segment 3: The Three Growth Loops Every B2B Company Needs [00:26:45]
Lenny: What are the primary growth loops you see working in modern B2B SaaS?

Elena Verna: There are three loops:
1. Casual Contact Loop: A user creates something in your tool and shares it with a non-user. For example, a Miro board link sent to an agency client, or a Loom recording sent to an executive. The recipient experiences the value and signs up.
2. Collaboration Loop: The product literally cannot be used without inviting someone else. Think Figma multiplayer or Google Docs commenting.
3. Content Loop: Content generated by users inside the product is indexed publicly on search engines or social media (e.g. Canva templates, Notion public pages).

If you don't have at least one of these loops working, PLG will fail, and you will just be paying Facebook or Google ads for user acquisition.

---

### Segment 4: The Sales-Assist Model [00:39:10]
Lenny: When do you bring sales into a PLG motion?

Elena Verna: You bring sales in when Product-Qualified Leads (PQLs) hit critical mass inside an account. Do not let sales cold-call people who just signed up 10 minutes ago! Sales should only engage when an account has 5+ active users collaborating, or when usage hits a security threshold (SSO, SOC2, admin control needs). We call this the Product-Led Sales or Sales-Assist motion. Sales becomes an accelerator of existing demand rather than a creator of artificial demand.
